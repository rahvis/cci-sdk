Both pipelines correctly recognized these as emergencies. The difference is what happened next: the baseline's own confidence number was enough, alone, to send emergency-level guidance straight to the patient. CCI's design never lets confidence alone make that call.

The actual guarantees returned, in plain terms

Urgency check: the calibrated candidate set contained the correct urgency level at least 80% of the time on the 48 cases it was checked against — a measured rate, not an opinion.
Auto-send check: among decisions this check would approve, the expected rate of being wrong is at most 15% — computed from held-out outcomes, not self-reported.
Reply-grounding check: at least 70% of the time, every sentence let through is actually backed by the patient's own intake facts or the assigned guidance — everything else gets dropped before it reaches anyone.
